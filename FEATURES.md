# Productivity Challenger Timer: 702 Features

Nov 11, 2026 · @winzo

## How to read this list

The app has one job: get you to start, every day, and prove it. Every feature below is built on a known behaviour principle, named in brackets where it helps.

Three design rules sit under all 702 points:

- **Procrastination is an emotion problem, not a character flaw.** You delay because the task feels bad right now, not because you are hopeless. So the app shrinks the bad feeling instead of shouting at you.
- **Starting beats duration.** A 2-minute start counts as a win. Long hours grow from daily starts, never the other way round.
- **Proof beats feeling.** Every session ends with something saved, so on a bad day you can look at evidence instead of trusting your mood.

**On the 110-hour week.** The app includes sprint modes for high intensity, but it will not push 110 hours as a daily standard. Research on long hours generally finds that output per hour falls sharply past roughly 50 hours a week, and lost sleep hurts learning most. For a student, a 1-crore outcome comes from skill hours that compound over years. So the app aims for intensity you can repeat for 1,000 days, with sleep protected.

**Build order.** Do not build all 702. Start with the 18 features in Version 1 below, then unlock the rest by section.

## The force stack

Seven layers run through the day, each one harder to ignore than the last. You set all of them yourself in advance, when you are thinking clearly.

| Layer | When | What happens | Principle | Features |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Night before | Tomorrow's first task and cue time are locked | Precommitment | 188 |
| 2 | Cue time | Full-screen alarm with a "Start 2 min" button | Cue with zero friction | 470, 471 |
| 3 | Until first session | Entertainment apps stay locked | Earn your phone | 201, 213 |
| 4 | All day | A bonus you already hold disappears at day end if you skip | Loss aversion | 248, 456 |
| 5 | Evening | Your buddy gets the day's result automatically, including a zero | Social accountability | 327 |
| 6 | Weekly | A small stake or forfeit you chose is triggered by a missed target | Commitment contract | 336, 337, 338 |
| 7 | 9 pm | Call-style rescue alert for a 2-minute save | Last chance | 67, 472 |

**Safety valve.** Freezes and the never-miss-twice rule (51, 52) mean one bad day costs little. Without this, a harsh system gets deleted after the first failure.

**Delayed off switch.** Any lock or stake can be turned off in settings, but the change takes 24 hours to apply. You stay in control, and you cannot quit in a weak moment.

**Against Duolingo.** Duolingo runs mainly on streaks, loss aversion, reminders and surprise rewards. This app uses all four and adds three stronger ones: your phone stays locked until you work, a real person sees your result, and a session counts only with proof. No one can promise "100 times more addictive," and that is not the target. The app is hooked to work done, not to opening the app, so every reward is paid for a session or proof and never for scrolling inside it.

## 1. Getting in the door (1 to 25)

These features remove the effort of starting, which is where most delay happens.

- **1. One-tap start.** The app opens on a single Start button. No menus before the first action.
- **2. Two-minute door.** The default first session is 2 minutes. Finishing it counts as a full win.
- **3. Just-open streak.** Opening the app before a set hour is itself a streak.
- **4. Five-second launch.** A 5-4-3-2-1 countdown starts the timer automatically, so there is no time to argue with yourself.
- **5. Next tiny step card.** Home shows one action only, written as a physical verb: "Open notebook to page 12."
- **6. If-then plans.** Create rules like "When I finish dinner, I open the app." The app reminds you at the cue. (Implementation intentions)
- **7. Habit stacking.** Attach a session to a routine you already have, like morning tea or the college bus.
- **8. Temptation bundling.** A favourite playlist plays only during sessions.
- **9. Start ritual.** The same 20-second sequence every time (sound, one breath, tap) becomes the cue to work.
- **10. Resume where I left.** Start screen shows the last note you wrote to yourself.
- **11. Parking note.** At session end, write one line: the exact next step for future you.
- **12. Ugly first draft mode.** The session goal is "make it bad on purpose." Kills perfectionism.
- **13. Setup-only session.** The only goal is to open the files and clear the desk.
- **14. Dice start.** Cannot choose? The app rolls among today's three tasks.
- **15. Bad-day bar.** A slider drops the daily minimum to 1 minute on rough days.
- **16. Home-screen widget.** Start button and streak number without opening the app.
- **17. Quick-settings tile.** Start a session from the phone's pull-down panel.
- **18. Voice start.** "Start five minutes on DSA."
- **19. NFC desk tag.** Tap your phone on a sticker at your desk to start.
- **20. Location cue.** Arriving at the library offers a session.
- **21. Charger cue.** Plugging in at your desk triggers a start prompt.
- **22. Morning first win.** One 2-minute task before any other app is opened.
- **23. Start count.** Lifetime "times started" is the biggest number on the home screen.
- **24. Starting-delay meter.** Tracks the gap between planned start and real start. The goal is to shrink it.
- **25. No-zero day.** Any single logged action keeps the day from being a zero.

## Persuasion features: the rules for sections 25 to 28

These 101 features use the same persuasion techniques that social and gaming apps use to hold attention, pointed at your own goals and switched on by you.

- **Used:** loss aversion, ownership, sunk cost, commitment and consistency, defaults, social proof, status, reciprocity, scarcity, curiosity and unpredictable rewards.
- **Left out:** shame, fear, fake numbers and anything that cuts sleep. Shame and self-blame tend to increase procrastination, so they would make the app work worse.
- **Real data only.** Any count of other people must be true. A fake "37 people are working" breaks trust the first time you notice.
